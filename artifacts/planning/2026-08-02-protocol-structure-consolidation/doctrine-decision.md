# Technique folds — doctrine decision record

> Decision record · 2026-08-02 · **Status:** Settled (per-edge disposition pending the W2 guard inventory)

Settles the doctrine question W3 of the protocol-structure epic (#397) left open: the design canon forbids technique→technique work calls in three homes while the addressing spec fully defines the call syntax and the corpus carries 118 live edges, with no mechanical enforcement on either side. Decided 2026-08-02 across five review rounds — an eight-question founding interview, a second review of fold form and wrap logic, a gap review (rows 9–13), a refinement round (rows 9 and 12 amended; rows 14–15; the #405 ancestry direction), and a rollout-governance round (rows 16–19). Every round ran as options with a recommendation each; every recommendation was adopted except the guard-landing severity (row 16), where a stricter option than recommended was chosen. Nineteen decision rows total, plus the #405 direction recorded in the Amendments. Machinery evidence for the claims below lives in the prior investigation folder, [2026-08-02-inline-technique-fold-investigation](../2026-08-02-inline-technique-fold-investigation/).

## The decision in one line

**Sanction declared folds, bounded by the visibility rule:** a technique may call another technique when the call's outcome stays inside the caller's produce path; any edge whose outcome the workflow itself acts on — a gate reads it, a checkpoint presents it, a loop retries it, a dispatch depends on it — must be an activity step bind.

## Rationale — why the visibility rule is the line

Every runtime affordance the server has is keyed to the activity/step boundary: delivery and the eager-bundle budget, `get_technique`'s step-keyed addressing, the delivery ledger's agent scoping, binding-seam provenance, step-manifest fidelity (Layer 5), and every mechanical guard. A call edge gains those affordances only by surfacing as a step; no composition mechanism can make an edge participate in gates, manifests, loops, or checkpoints from inside a step boundary. That cost is constitutive, not implementational — which is what makes it the sort key.

Conversely, once the call seam is *checked*, the fold and the activity bind are near-isomorphic server-side: the seam must carry callee reference, input bindings, and output mappings — the `{name, inputs, outputs}` triple of the activity step bind, relocated into composed delivery. What actually differs is who can see the edge. So the doctrine question reduces to, per edge: **who consumes the outcome?** Workflow consumes it → step (visibility is the point). Caller consumes it → fold (encapsulation is the point; lifting fragments the produce path). Nobody executes it → documentation reference (guarded name, no fold).

The unchecked status quo was never a third option: the corpus measurements (118 edges, 84% invisible to the activity layer, 56 call sites omitting a required callee input, live argument renames) are the unchecked form's track record, and the prohibition without a checker produced them.

## Accepted residual risks

1. **Wrap-only invariants do not travel** (decision 5) — **retired 2026-08-02, surveyed empty.** The pre-delivery survey ran: zero containers across all 16 workflows author an `Initial` or `Final` block. The wrap machinery is built and runs on every composition but currently composes nothing; containers express themselves entirely through Rules and shared I/O. (The only container `## Protocol` in the corpus is an unnumbered `template-selection` block on work-package's `update-pr` — parent-only, never wrapped, and already slated for a W1 refile.) Nothing is lost at adoption; the risk exists only for future containers and is covered by the W1 title reservation recorded in the Amendments.
2. **Mostly-internal, occasionally-gated values** (decisions 1 and 4): an edge whose output is internal in most walks but gated in one caller sits on the line. The warn-level lint (not hard-fail) is the accommodation; disposition is per-edge judgment recorded in the W3 inventory.
3. **Fold gravity** (decision 4): folds are the lower-ceremony option, so edges will drift toward techniques over time. The two-direction lint is the standing counterweight; if drift shows up anyway, the "step in disguise" warning is the escalation candidate for hard-fail.

## Amendments — 2026-08-02 second review (fold form and wrap logic)

A follow-up review re-derived the fold-form choice against the wrap implementation and delivered a worked example. No decision flips; two are amended above (rows 2 and 5, and residual risk 1), on this evidence:

1. **The wrap machinery has zero corpus users.** `wrapProtocolWithAncestors` runs on every composition, but no container `TECHNIQUE.md` in any of the 16 workflows authors an `Initial` or `Final` block. Two consequences: residual risk 1 retires empty, and the "in-house splice precedent" argument for textual splicing loses its footing — the platform's only exercised precedent for delivering foreign content into a composition is the marked, hashed block (`inherited_inputs`/`inherited_outputs`), not the splice.
2. **Cross-workflow ancestry is inconsistent between the two delivery paths** (filed as #405). The bundle path (`resolveTechniques`, nested branch) composes an op with the containers of the workflow where the file was found (`nestedDir` from `opWorkflow`) — a meta group op arrives with its group's rules. The step-bound path (`composeTechnique`, used by step-bound `get_technique` and hybrid bundling) resolves ancestors from the executing workflow's tree (`techniquesDir` from `workflowId`), where the source group does not exist — the same rules silently don't travel. Spec §5 documents executing-only; the `composeLoaded`/`composeTechnique` comments claim the paths identical. Decision 5's fold carriage therefore names **source ancestry** explicitly: the fold regularises one behavior rather than inheriting an ambiguity, and it fixes an existing under-delivery on the step-bound path rather than preserving it.
3. **The splice steelman, resolved.** Substitution-at-composition genuinely eliminates runtime misbinding — the strongest argument for splice, and the original α-renaming objection understated it (renaming only ever concerned locals; bound I/O substitutes cleanly). The block design keeps that advantage by having the server compute and print the seam bindings, while splice's costs are structural and unrecoverable: identity loss (dedup, ledger collapse, telemetry), rules-scope collapse, and compounding variance under transitive folds. Row 2's rejected-alternatives rationale is refreshed accordingly.
4. **W1 grammar collision, new finding.** W1 makes an *unnumbered* protocol sub-section mean a named variant, while spec §6 reserves the two unnumbered titles `Initial`/`Final` for the ancestor wrap. Zero current users makes this free to settle now: W1 reserves the two titles out of variant interpretation. Deprecating the wrap outright is a costed option on the same evidence (zero users, one inconsistent cross-workflow story, and its conceptual job — invariant framing — served better by rules per decision 5); that call belongs to the epic owner.

## Amendments — 2026-08-02 gap review (decisions 9–13 and enforcement wording)

A completeness pass over the settled doctrine surfaced five decision-shaped gaps — now rows 9–13 — plus enforcement wording and bookkeeping, settled here:

**Enforcement wording**

- **Engine-gate exclusion (row 6)** — restated as lint-plus-disposition; the size cap is the mechanically enforced half. Acceptance language reads "the size cap is enforced, with the gate exclusion applied through the boundary lint and the per-edge disposition".
- **Unmapped callee outputs** warn as dead-at-site (ratchet-consistent, matching the corpus's `pushed_branch` case); **two folds landing the same name** in one caller namespace is a hard error.
- **A rule addressed with call syntax** (`Apply group::rule-name(args)`) becomes a load error once prose is promoted to structure; the corpus holds three known sites (the `infrastructure-submodule-paths` spellings in meta's version-control group) for W2 to flag.
- **Disposition gains a fourth outcome — deduplicate.** 19 of the 118 edges are also step-bound; those resolve by deleting or demoting the inline side, and a fold duplicating a sibling step bind in the same activity is flagged the way duplicate steps already are.
- **W2 consequence of row 9**: the guard reproduces the 118-edge inventory and *classifies* the 56 argument-omitting sites into name-match-satisfied versus genuinely unbound — only the second class is drift.

**Bookkeeping folded into scope**

- Canon: AP-124's Fix currently prescribes rules-as-variant-slices — the exact workaround W1 retires — and is rewritten with W1; AP-51/53/54/56 change status from prose-only to partially mechanical once the parser and guard land.
- Docs: the resolution-model doc gains a fold section; the fidelity doc gains the fold delivery event; the API reference gains the fold response fields.
- W3 scope: folds deliver on all three surfaces (step-bound `get_technique`, `get_activity` bundling, the `get_workflow` orchestrator bundle); fold collection runs post-variant-selection, so unselected-variant folds never ship; fold bodies draw the same cumulative eager budget, the whole step falling back to lazy on overflow; fold-site addressing stays stable across protocol renumbering (ledger and event keys).

**Accepted residual (row 9)**: name-match can bind a coincidentally same-named caller symbol with different meaning — semantics no lint can check. Mitigation is the server-printed seam (the resolved binding is visible at the site) and review; recorded as a known residual rather than a checkable rule.

## Amendments — 2026-08-02 rounds four and five (refinements and rollout governance)

Round four amended rows 9 and 12 in place (bag fallthrough for input name-match; the `protocol:` selector name), added rows 14–15, and settled the #405 direction. Round five added rows 16–19. Notes that don't fit a table cell:

1. **#405 direction: home-tree ancestry.** A technique's containers are the ones physically above its file — its group and home workflow root — wherever it executes. Both delivery doors converge on what the activity-bundle door already does; spec §5's executing-only sentence is the text that yields; the borrowing workflow's context reaches a borrowed op through the caller's composition and the session, never by grafting foreign ancestry onto the file. Implementation stays on #405 (align both paths, fix the routine's comments, add the both-doors comparison test).
2. **Evidence-settled, no decision needed:** argument keys are **bare** (spec §4.2 and all corpus call sites agree; principle 16 and the paren-args entry's italic clause amend in the W3 canon pass); no corpus input is named `protocol` or `variant`; the meta root's Capability overpromises ("Inputs, Outputs, Rules, and Errors" over an Inputs-only file) — one-line fix candidate.
3. **Convergence payoff worth engineering for:** with the wrap removed (row 15) and home ancestry settled, a fold body and a step-bound body of the same op compose byte-identically — folds therefore share the `technique:` delivery-ledger channel (the hybrid-bundling precedent), and cross-door collapse comes free.
4. **W3 specification worklist** (shapes obvious, no interview): runtime-unresolvable folds surface through the existing `unresolved` contract (marker, never silent, never a session failure — the guard makes this unreachable in a clean corpus); the agent emits a `▶ fold <site>` execution beat mirroring the bundled-step beat contract; namespace precedence for name-match follows textual order (locals and outputs satisfy a match only when produced before the site); a seam landing on an already-declared name is a redeclaration error, with the mutually-exclusive-branch exception extended to seams; folding a container with no protocol is a load error; the paren grammar formalizes quoted strings, numbers, booleans, and designators (nothing else); an unbound optional input falls to its declared default; fold-site addressing stays stable across renumbering; `protocol` and `variant` are reserved against future op input ids.
