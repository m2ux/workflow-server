**Component:** config/

## Overview

The corpus audit moves to this project's declaration. workflow-server states its corpus-audit criterion in `config/required-criteria.json`, and the skill's guide states only the mechanism a project fills in.

## Problem

A generic guide carries workflow-server's corpus doctrine, so another project inherits a requirement it cannot satisfy.

- The Corpus Audit section of `epic-issue-body.md` fixes the criterion's wording, its row's name and the branch that brings it.
- The Dispatch step of `deliver-mode.md` and the deferred-debt epic of `initiative-issue-body.md` link that section, so delivery confirms a corpus audit on every project.
- This repository declares nothing, so since W02 the format check reports its required criteria unevaluable.

## Proposal

The format check reads a project's required criteria from `config/required-criteria.json` at the evaluated commit, and the guide's Required Criteria section names workflow-server's corpus audit only as a labelled Example.

- **This project declares its corpus audit.**
  `config/required-criteria.json` holds one entry: branch `workflows`, the canon-audit criterion, phrase `canon audit`, row `Canon audit of the changed definitions`. It is the guide's Example, entry for entry.
- **The guide states the mechanism alone.**
  The Corpus Audit section leaves `epic-issue-body.md`. What holds for any required criterion moves into Required Criteria: the row that delivers it depends on the rows whose work reaches its branch, covers that criterion alone, and is a unit of its own at dispatch.
- **Callers name the mechanism.**
  The Dispatch step of `deliver-mode.md` confirms each epic carries the required criteria its rows bring. The deferred-debt epic of `initiative-issue-body.md` carries the required criteria its project declares for the branches its rows reach.
- **A guard keeps the vocabulary in the declaration.**
  A test finds the project's vocabulary nowhere in the skill's prose but the Required Criteria Example.
