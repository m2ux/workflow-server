## Overview

`respond_checkpoint` resolves a checkpoint by a selected option or by the auto-advance its definition declares, and by nothing else. The descriptions a caller reads say those two ways, and the answer records the option that was taken.

## Problem

- **The response tool takes a third way through a checkpoint.**
  `respond_checkpoint` takes `option_id`, `auto_advance`, or `condition_not_met`. A true `condition_not_met` clears the gate with no option selected.
- **Dismissal is written into the descriptions a caller reads.**
  The response tool names dismissal in eight places, the step schema in four, and the loader in one.
  Schema text names `condition` as what makes a checkpoint dismissible, the tool text says to pass `condition_not_met`, and the loader names a dismissed checkpoint.
- **The dismissal leaves a sentinel record and a response field.**
  The session stores the answer under `__condition_not_met__`, and both `respond_checkpoint` and `get_workflow_status` return `dismissed`.

## Proposal

A checkpoint resolves by a selected option or by the auto-advance its definition declares. The step schemas keep a structured `condition`. Their descriptions, the tool descriptions, and the loader message stop treating that field as a way to clear a checkpoint without a decision.

- **One of two inputs.**
  `option_id` selects an option. `auto_advance` selects the checkpoint's `defaultOption` once `autoAdvanceMs` has elapsed. A call that supplies both, or neither, is refused. `reply` accompanies `option_id`.
- **The answer names the option.**
  The recorded response and the status of the last checkpoint carry the option id. There is no `condition_not_met` input, no `__condition_not_met__` record, and no `dismissed` field.
- **Descriptions state the two ways.**
  The tool description, the published parameter hints, the schema descriptions, and the loader's default-exit message say how a checkpoint is answered. None describes dismissing a checkpoint, and none names a gate field as what would permit that.
