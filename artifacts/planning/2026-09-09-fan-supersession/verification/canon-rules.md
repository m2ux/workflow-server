# Rules the fan makes redundant — verified

**Refutation pass.** Read both design documents in full at the cited regions, then read every named construct whole and measured its own blast radius with grep rather than trusting the stated one.

**Counts I reproduced.** 26 activity files declare `scatter-gather`, every one in an activity's own `techniques:` list; zero `workflow.yaml` declares it in any bucket, so no orchestrator ever receives it. `dispatch-workers` is bound 15 times across 7 files — but the split matters and the finder never made it: **10 of the 15 sit in three activities bound in live graphs** (`cicd-pipeline-security-audit/activities/03-primary-scan.yaml` ×4, `substrate-node-security-audit/activities/02-reconnaissance.yaml` ×2, `.../03-primary-audit.yaml` ×4), and the other 5 sit in `meta/activities/patterns/` templates that **nothing in the corpus borrows** — `grep -rn 'meta/patterns/' --include=*.yaml` returns zero hits. `classify-request` is bound only inside `02-supervisor.yaml`, so it has no live caller at all. No harness technique file (`claude-code.md`, `cursor.md`, `cline.md`, `generic.md`) states any depth or nesting limit, so `depth-1-only`'s closing citation points at nothing.

**Where the finder was wrong.** One candidate is withdrawn outright: AP-128's Detect is anchored on a *gated producer* and traverses forward to readers, so a fan parameter with no producer at all is never reached — the exposure the finder describes belongs to `binding-fidelity`'s orphan-input check, which stage 4 already teaches. Six verdicts came down: `dispatch-workers` (REMOVE→DEPRECATE, ten live bindings survive and `binding-fidelity`'s arg-conformance check hard-fails the moment `dispatch_concurrency` goes while `03-primary-scan.yaml:29` still binds it); §18's sentence and the three inventory rows (REMOVE→DEPRECATE, each keeps a job at the in-worker layer and the inventory's universal obligation means a deleted row strands an informal pattern); the patterns README (REMOVE→DEPRECATE, its in-activity-only claim survives as the fence); and four all the way to KEEP — `one-advance-per-activity`, `depth-1-only`, `orchestration-patterns::isolation-then-combine` and `outputs-by-name-and-path`.

**The most load-bearing correction.** `one-advance-per-activity` is a KEEP, not a DEPRECATE, and the design's subsumption claim at README:1076 / T3 is false. I reproduced the mechanism in live code: both callers omit `from_activity` (`continue-batch.md:46`, `dispatch-activity.md:51`), the design's own resolver step 1 takes "the sole entry" when unnamed, and `src/tools/workflow-tools.ts:770-774` records `draft.currentActivity` as exited and completed before line 842 assigns `activity_id` — with no comparison between the two. A second advance onto the activity already current still silently records false completion after the fan lands.

**And the correction that reverses a rationale.** AP-110's fan-out carve-out does not lose its subject — it gains one. The fan leaves two layers standing (session-level graph dispatch, now `dispatch-activity` *and* `dispatch-fan`; work units inside one worker), so the carve-out must name the new op rather than go. Only its Detect arm's remedy pointer is stale.

**One adjacent site neither the finder nor the plan's sweep reaches:** `prism/workflow.yaml:15` carries a `rules.workflow` entry mandating a fresh sub-agent per analytical pass via `harness-compat::spawn-agent`. That is executable (orchestrator bucket) and is a hand-written session-level fan the graph would own after this lands — the design's Tier 3, and precisely what AP-110's Detect arm catches once repointed.

A verdict of REMOVE means the fan does this job now and leaving the construct means two paths do one job. DEPRECATE means it keeps a caller for the moment. KEEP means it looked superseded and is not — those are the entries a confident implementer would delete by mistake.

### REMOVE — workflows/meta/techniques/scatter-gather.md — `## Rules` → `one-gather-contract-two-scatter-modes`, plus the Parallel arms of Protocol steps 1 and 2

**Why the fan takes it.** The rule's whole content is that sequential accumulation and parallel fan-out are one primitive differing only in dispatch mechanism, and the Protocol's parallel arm is the dispatch half of that claim. I confirmed the parallel arm has no reachable audience: all 26 declarations are activity-level, no workflow bucket carries the technique, and the arm applies `spawn-concurrent`, which `depth-1-only` denies a spawned agent. The fan gives concurrency a home in the graph, and at graph grain the two modes are no longer one primitive — an instance fan's alternative is an in-activity `forEach`, a different construct in a different file with an inverted cost profile.

**Evidence.** scatter-gather.md:8 (Capability, "one primitive, two scatter modes"), :12-17 (Protocol, both mode arms), :22-24 (the rule). `grep -rn 'scatter-gather' --include=*.yaml` → 26 hits, all under an activity's own `techniques:` key; zero under any `workflow.yaml`. `meta/workflow.yaml:13-21` shows `techniques.workflow` is the orchestrator bucket and holds no scatter-gather. spawn-agent.md:44-46. Design README:385, :1294, :1459. delivery-plan.md:20 lists the file in stage 4; :51 states what stage 4 adds; :306 retires the paired parallel selection on `dispatch-workers`.

**Blast radius.** **Smaller than stated.** No file cites `one-gather-contract-two-scatter-modes` by name — `grep -rn 'one-gather-contract-two-scatter-modes'` returns only its own heading, so removing it breaks zero anchors. The three `::rule-name` citations the finder listed belong to the two neighbouring rules, not this one. `anti-patterns.md:490` (AP-33 do-not-flag) and `:560` (AP-39 coverage discriminator) name the technique but not this rule. The 26 activity declarations are untouched: they use the sequential arm.

**If left alone.** Two authored ways to say "run this N times concurrently" — one in the graph that executes, one in a strategy technique delivered to 26 activities that cannot — plus a rule whose stated purpose is keeping them substitutable. An author who reads the rule first authors the mode that never runs.

**Changed under scrutiny.** Narrowed two claims and enlarged one. The finder's argument that "the `concurrency = 1` case is unrepresentable" because the schema refuses a fan of one is **wrong** — the in-activity `forEach` loop *is* the concurrency-1 shape, it needs no agent, it runs everywhere, and it survives untouched. What actually fails is the equivalence: after the fan the two "modes" sit at different layers (a loop step inside an activity versus a graph destination), so nothing is substitutable for anything. Enlarged: the design and the plan *keep* the parallel mode and add the graph fan as a third mode over the same gather contract (README:2068, delivery-plan:51), which is what makes this a finding against the plan rather than something the plan already schedules. Blast radius corrected from three citations to zero.

### REMOVE — workflows/meta/techniques/scatter-gather.md — `## Rules` → `parallelism-is-optimisation`

**Why the fan takes it.** The entry exists only to name a default between two dispatch modes.
