# What the design itself should not be adding — verified

**Refutation pass.** Fifteen candidates, all reproduced from the repository; six survive, eight are refuted down to KEEP, one is withdrawn. The refutations cluster around a single mistake: reading \"the fan can express this shape\" as \"the fan takes this job\". Three of the KEEPs fall to the trap named in the brief — scatter-gather's sequential mode is an in-context forEach that needs no agent and is bound as an activity-level strategy technique on roughly twenty-two activities across seven workflows, and the two rules that license it (`one-gather-contract-two-scatter-modes`, `parallelism-is-optimisation`) carry three live anchor refs and are the doctrine spawn-agent.md:46 cites to make a worker's sequential scatter conformance rather than shortfall. Parallel mode is not unexecutable either: that same line says parallel scatter is available at the orchestrator, and the fan's own dispatch operation is an instance of it. Two more KEEPs (maxInstances, the frontier conversion in the legacy converter) are constructs the unbuilt design introduces, not existing features, and both survive on the merits: maxInstances is the fan's `maxIterations` — a per-site ceiling the schema already carries in 45 corpus bindings with no server default at all — and it is the only mitigation the design's own residual-risk table offers for a meeting point that re-pays full delivery. One KEEP inverts the finder's own hazard: in `getActivity`, exact-match-first is what protects an id containing the separator, so removing it introduces a silent mis-resolution rather than removing one. And `activity_id` on `next_activity` is not a second home for a derived fact — finalize-activity.md step 2 resolves routing inside the worker and forbids omitting it (\"the delivery they were resolved from reached this context and no other\"), so `validateReportedExit` reconciles an agent's routing decision against the graph; delete one side and the check goes, not a redundancy. The single most consequential correction runs the other way. The design claims T3 subsumes the second-advance hazard `one-advance-per-activity` names. It does not: dispatch-activity.md step 2 and continue-batch.md step 1 both call `next_activity` without naming an exiting activity, so on the exact sequence the rule forbids, the resolver's \"unnamed with at most one entry\" arm silently takes the just-entered activity, retires it as complete before a worker has walked a step, and re-enters it. That makes the prose rule a KEEP and makes required `from_activity` a REMOVE that is load-bearing rather than cosmetic — two candidates the finder filed separately are one finding, and only one of them can be closed by removal.

A verdict of REMOVE means the fan does this job now and leaving the construct means two paths do one job. DEPRECATE means it keeps a caller for the moment. KEEP means it looked superseded and is not — those are the entries a confident implementer would delete by mistake.

### REMOVE — workflows/meta/techniques/orchestration-patterns/dispatch-workers.md — the `dispatch_concurrency` input and its greater-than-1 protocol branch (step 3, the spawn-concurrent batch)

**Why the fan takes it.** The graph fan places orchestrator-level concurrent fan-out where the dispatch primitive actually is. Every one of the operation's 15 bindings sits in an activity a dispatched worker executes, and `spawn-agent`'s depth rule says a spawned agent inherits no dispatch primitive — so the concurrent branch has never been reachable from a single call site. Once the fan lands, the corpus documents two ways to say "run these N pieces of work at once", and the one an author meets first cannot run.

**Evidence.** dispatch-workers.md:16-18 declares the input verbatim as the finder quotes it; :29-30 are the two protocol branches. I counted the operation's bindings independently: substrate-node-security-audit/activities/03-primary-audit.yaml (48, 75, 81, 87), 02-reconnaissance.yaml (37, 49), cicd-pipeline-security-audit/activities/03-primary-scan.yaml (27, 50, 56, 62), meta/activities/patterns/05-lead-researcher.yaml (47, 76), 01-orchestrator-workers.yaml (42), 02-supervisor.yaml (48), 04-isolated-fan-out.yaml (47) — 15 across 7 files, matching the design. The disqualifier is reproducible without the design doc: spawn-agent.md:44 (`depth-1-only`) and :46 ("Parallel scatter is available only where the dispatch primitive is — at the orchestrator"), against docs/dispatch-model.md, which states that workers execute activities and orchestrators dispatch them. The one live concurrency binding is cicd-pipeline-security-audit/activities/03-primary-scan.yaml:29 `dispatch_concurrency: scanners_assigned`, with `scanners_assigned` declared at :9 — the shadow width variable stage 7 deletes.

**Blast radius.** Corrected downward from the finder in one place and upward in another. The 15 binding lines and the two reader-side declarations (orchestration-patterns/TECHNIQUE.md:16-18 and :62) are right. `harness-compat/spawn-concurrent.md` does NOT lose its only caller: scatter-gather.md:14 still invokes it, and dispatch-fan will. The upward correction matters more: the plan's fallback position — delivery-plan.md:306, "the operation states its in-activity sequential contract positively" — describes a contract the operation does not have. Its sequential branch is `spawn-agent` once per brief (dispatch-workers.md:29), which is itself a dispatch primitive, so it is equally unexecutable at all 15 sites. Retiring only the parallel selection leaves an operation that still cannot run anywhere it is bound.

**If left alone.** Two documented paths for one job, one of them unreachable, plus a maintained rule telling each reader which layer it may use — the shape the repo's own principle says to design out. And `dispatch_concurrency` stays live as a second authored home for a fan's width, against the design's own "the width bound has one home".

**Changed under scrutiny.** I widened the claim rather than narrowing it. The finder treated the sequential arm as a viable survivor; it is not — `spawn-agent` per brief is a dispatch too, so the whole operation is unexecutable at every binding, and the plan's "state the sequential contract positively" remedy would ship a positively-stated contract that is still dead. I also corrected the spawn-concurrent claim: it keeps a caller.

**Changed under scrutiny.** 02-supervisor does not belong in the fan's account. ### REMOVE — The optional `from_activity` parameter on `next_activity` and the sole-entry fallback in `heldActivity(state, named)` (src/utils/session/resolver.ts, per README.md:1012)

**Why the fan takes it.** The optionality exists so existing sequential callers need no edit, which the repo's principles put on the same footing as a fallback. But the stronger reason is one the finder did not reach: requiring the parameter is what actually delivers the subsumption the design claims for the second-advance hazard. With it optional, the server guesses the sole frontier entry on exactly the call sequence the corpus polices with prose.

**Evidence.** README.md:1052 carries the description verbatim ("Omit while one activity is in flight; required while a fan is running"); :1058 step 1 carries four cases, two of which exist only to handle omission; :1006-1012 states the guess. Reproduced against the live drive loop: dispatch-activity.md step 2 calls `next_activity { session_index, activity_id, step_manifest }` with no exiting-activity parameter, and continue-batch.md step 1 does the same. So on the sequence continue-batch advances to B, then dispatch-activity advances to B again, the design's rule resolves "unnamed with at most one entry" to the sole entry B, retires B, and re-enters B — the hazard, unrefused. With `from_activity` required, that call names an activity already retired and T3 refuses it, which is the design's claim made true.

**Blast radius.** The parameter declaration, `heldActivity`'s signature and body, step 1 of the resolution rule, one of the four verbatim refusals, and the two definitions that compose the call — evaluate-transition.md and finalize-activity.md, both already on the stage-2 file list. Every existing `next_activity` test gains one argument. One genuine exception the parameter must keep: the session's first call has no exiting activity, so the honest contract is "required whenever the frontier is non-empty" — the same conditional shape T2 already uses for `exit`.

**If left alone.** The server guesses which activity a call is exiting on the code path that retires it, records its exit, applies its variable writes under a branch key and stamps its trace. Correctness rests on a count rather than on the call — and the `one-advance-per-activity` prohibition stays load-bearing prose because the guess keeps the hazard expressible.

**Changed under scrutiny.** I found the finder's case understated. It argued the optionality on aesthetic and rule-economy grounds; the measured consequence is that the guess is precisely what keeps the second-advance hazard alive under the frontier, so this candidate and the `one-advance-per-activity` candidate are the same finding seen from two ends — and only one of them can be resolved by removal.

### REMOVE — src/schema/session.schema.ts:99 — the session-level `exit` field, with its write at src/tools/workflow-tools.ts:843 and its slot in the canonical key ordering at src/utils/session/store.ts:123

**Evidence.** Declared at session.schema.ts:99 `exit: z.string().default('')`, doc-commented "Exit the last completed activity took, as its orchestrator reported it"; interface member at :207; initialiser at :285. **Blast radius.** One declaration, one interface member, one initialiser, one write, one canonical-ordering entry, and the legacy converter's reconstruction (migration.ts:187 and :245). **Changed under scrutiny.** I verified the absence of readers across the projections, the resolver, logging, resource tools, scripts, tests and the Python reference reader rather than accepting it, and I closed the one open risk the finder left — that dropping the key from the canonical ordering would break existing seals. It does not.

### REMOVE — src/utils/validation.ts:14-31 — the `act` member of `SessionView`, and `sessionView()` in src/utils/session/resolver.ts:132-138 which populates it from `state.currentActivity`

**Why the fan takes it.** `act` is a single-valued answer to "what is the run on", and the frontier replaces that question because the answer is plural. Its source field is deleted outright by the design ("a scalar kept beside the list would be a second home for the run's position"), so the projection cannot survive as written under any reading.

**Evidence.** resolver.ts:135 `act: state.currentActivity`, in a function whose doc comment says the view keeps the validation surface storage-agnostic. I measured every read: validation.ts:34, :40, :45, :49 in `validateActivityTransition` and :240, :241, :243, :248, :251 in `validateReportedExit` — nine reads, two functions, nowhere else (`rg 'view\.act' src/` returns exactly those nine). Both functions are called only from `next_activity`, at workflow-tools.ts:928 and :748. `sessionView` is called at eight sites in total (workflow-tools 604, 734, 1052, 1669, 1892, 2067; resource-tools 740, 888), and the other six use only `wf`/`v` via `validateWorkflowVersion`, so the interface survives with those two members.

**Blast radius.** Two functions, nine reads, one projection function, one interface member. Both call sites are in a handler that, by the design's own step 1, has already resolved the retiring activity before it validates — so the correct value is in hand at the call.

**If left alone.** A single-valued position projection outliving the field it projected, feeding the two checks the design itself records as silently disabling, in a hard-zero validation surface with no ledger.

**Changed under scrutiny.** I narrowed what the finding actually is. The finder framed it as an oversight — a projection left standing. It is a forced edit the design under-specifies: `state.currentActivity` is being deleted, so `act` must either be re-sourced from `heldActivity` or removed, and the design's file list ("no marginal edit beyond what the frontier itself requires") does not say which. That makes this a signature question, not a capability question — but the status quo is not among the options, so the finding stands.

### DEPRECATE — src/utils/session/migration.ts — `migratePlanningFolder` and the whole pre-Phase-5 converter, with the `workflow-state.json` envelope and legacy-token decode path

**Why the fan takes it.** It does not. The fan forces an edit to it — the converter reconstructs `currentActivity`, the field the frontier deletes, so stage 5 cannot leave it alone — and that forced edit is the occasion to ask whether a compatibility layer for a state shape two schema generations old belongs on the `start_session` path.

**Evidence.** migration.ts:2-23 states the contract including "Legacy token decode failure … is non-fatal: the converter falls back to a minimal session shape" — a fallback inside a migration; :184-186 reconstructs `currentTechnique` through a second fallback chain (`technique` → `skill` → `currentTechnique` → `currentSkill`); :244 writes `currentActivity`. 414 lines. Invoked at resource-tools.ts:211 on the `start_session` path. tests/migration.test.ts is 230 lines with 14 `migratePlanningFolder` call sites, not 5. The legacy envelope's schema is `WorkflowStateSchema` in state.schema.ts:155-186, and I measured its live consumers: exactly one, scripts/generate-schemas.ts:6 and :26, which publishes it as a JSON schema. `scripts/count-workflow-sessions.ts` exists and is the instrument that would answer whether any pre-Phase-5 folder remains.

**Blast radius.** Corrected. src/schema/state.schema.ts is NOT legacy-only and cannot come out with the converter — it also holds `HistoryEventTypeSchema`, `HistoryEntrySchema` and `CheckpointResponseSchema`, all live and consumed by session.schema.ts and validation.ts. What is legacy-only inside it is `WorkflowStateSchema` and its neighbours (`createInitialState`, `validateState`, `safeValidateState`, `addHistoryEvent`, `LoopStateSchema`, `NestedWorkflowStateSchema`), whose sole consumer is the schema generator. The rest of the finder's list holds: migration.ts entire, resource-tools.ts:26 and :211, tests/migration.test.ts, and the `workflow-state.json` references in scripts/generate-session-token.ts and tests/mcp-server.test.ts. Honest cost, unmeasurable from here: a folder holding only `workflow-state.json` can no longer be resumed.

**If left alone.** The design pays an edit into a 414-line compatibility layer on the first call of every session, whose legacy envelope schema exists only to be published as JSON. If no pre-Phase-5 folder remains — and the burden is measurement, not assumption — the fan's edit is being made to code that should be gone.

**Changed under scrutiny.** I corrected the blast radius (state.schema.ts is mixed, not legacy; the test has 14 call sites, not 5) and measured the legacy envelope's only live consumer. I also want the causal link named honestly: this is the weakest link in the set. The fan does not supersede the converter, it only forces a touch, so this is a pre-existing debt whose removal the repo's own first principle justifies independently of anything in this design.
