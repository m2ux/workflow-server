## REMOVE — src/schema/session.schema.ts — `currentActivity` on `SessionFileBaseSchema` (line 96), the `SessionFile` interface (line 205) and `createInitialSessionFile` (line 283); plus its entry in `TOP_LEVEL_KEY_PRIORITY` at src/utils/session/store.ts:121

**What it does today.** The single slot holding what the run is on. Written once per transition (`draft.currentActivity = activity_id`, workflow-tools.ts:842) and read by every handler that needs to know where the session stands — manifest validation, the missing-manifest advisory, the reported-exit check, the exiting-activity attribution for variable writes and step-completed events, `get_activity`'s body selection, `get_technique`'s scope and step resolution, `yield_checkpoint`'s checkpoint lookup, `dispatch_child`'s `triggeredFrom`, the trace stamp, and three inspection projections.

**Why the fan takes it.** A fan puts several activities in flight at once, so the run's position stops being a scalar. The frontier is that position, and the design states the reason for replacement rather than coexistence: "The frontier is the run's only cursor: a scalar kept beside the list would be a second home for the run's position." Every one of the 53 references is asking "what is this call about", which the frontier plus `heldActivity(state, named)` answers correctly and the scalar answers only when one thing is in flight.

**Evidence.** Declared at src/schema/session.schema.ts:96 (`currentActivity: z.string().default('')`), 205 and 283. Measured 53 occurrences of the record's field across nine src files: src/tools/resource-tools.ts (24), src/tools/workflow-tools.ts (14), src/schema/session.schema.ts (3), src/utils/session/migration.ts (2), and one each in src/utils/session/params.ts, resolver.ts, store.ts, src/utils/validation.ts, src/logging.ts. A further 22 occurrences in tests/ and scripts/. The 6 occurrences in src/schema/state.schema.ts and the 4 `currentActivityId` parameters in src/utils/binding-provenance.ts are different constructs and are excluded from that count (state.schema.ts is its own row below).

**Blast radius.** src/tools/workflow-tools.ts:208, 261, 335 (the identity, activity and children projections — output-contract field names), 736-744 (step-manifest and technique-fetch validation gates), 747 (reported-exit gate), 770 (`exitingActivity`), 842 (the write), 1009 (`get_activity`), 1634 (`yield_checkpoint`), 2179 (`get_workflow_status`); src/tools/resource-tools.ts:387, 549, 556, 598, 605, 642-657, 662-704, 718-781, 901-910; src/utils/session/resolver.ts:135 (`sessionView`); src/utils/session/store.ts:121 (canonical ordering, which feeds the seal); src/utils/session/migration.ts:182, 243; src/logging.ts:104 (trace event); src/utils/session/params.ts:68 (a dead member of a structural type — its own row below); src/utils/validation.ts:17 (doc comment). **If left alone.** Two homes for the run's position, and the scalar is the one that lies: on a three-branch fan it holds whichever branch was written last, so `get_activity` serves one branch another branch's body, `get_technique` resolves a step id against the wrong activity, and the exiting-activity attribution lands one branch's variable writes and step-completed events under a sibling's name. Keeping it also means every barrier reading has two sources that can disagree, and the seal's canonical ordering carries a field nothing should be reading.

## REMOVE — src/utils/validation.ts:33-52 — `validateActivityTransition(view, workflow, activityId)`, and its sole helper src/loaders/workflow-loader.ts:506 `exitDestinations`

**What it does today.** Two unrelated advisory checks in one function. First: on the first call of a session (`!view.act`) the requested activity must equal `workflow.initialActivity`. Second: on any later call it warns when `activityId` is not among the dedup'd destinations of the current activity's bound exits — "Activity 'X' is not bound to any exit of 'Y'. The workflow graph sends its exits to: [...]". Called once, from `next_activity` (src/tools/workflow-tools.ts:928).

**Why the fan takes it.** The second check is a strictly weaker duplicate of `validateReportedExit`, and the fan removes the last case in which it was the only check. `validateReportedExit` (validation.ts:239-254) resolves the *named* exit's binding and warns when `binding.to !== activityId`; `validateActivityTransition` only asks whether the target appears among *some* exit's destinations. Whenever `exit` is supplied the two are logically nested — if the target is not in the destination set then the named binding cannot match either — so the same mistake produces two warnings with two wordings. The fan closes the gap that justified the weaker one: T2 makes `exit` required on any transition off a fanning exit, and T9 *refuses* a branch return whose named destination is not the derived meeting point, checked on every return. On a branch return the refusal throws in step 2 of the resolution rule, before any write, whereas `buildValidation(...)` runs at workflow-tools.ts:927 after the save at 870 — so the warning is unreachable on that path. On a fan enter the requested target is the whole destination, and the design would have to teach this function to "compare set-wise" for a comparison `validateReportedExit` already makes precisely. The `!view.act` arm is a separate concern that survives, but not where it is: once the frontier is the position, it is empty after the terminal sentinel retires ("the terminal sentinel retires and does not push"), so a stray `next_activity` on a completed session would newly warn "First activity must be '<initialActivity>'" — today `currentActivity` holds `__terminal__`, `exitDestinations` returns `[]`, and the function returns null.

**Evidence.** validation.ts:33-52 for the body; validation.ts:239-254 for `validateReportedExit`, whose message at line 251 ("Exit 'e' of 'A' is bound to 'B' but 'C' was requested") names the same defect as line 49. Both are wired at workflow-tools.ts:747-749 and 928 from the one handler. The delivery plan (line 33) keeps both and widens both: "satisfies a reported exit when the requested activity is among the destination's targets and compares set-wise on a fan enter, flattens the transition check" — two flattening implementations of one fact. The "exit omitted" case is already a contract violation on the orchestrator's side: workflows/meta/techniques/workflow-engine/finalize-activity.md:64 defines `activity_exit` as "The exit id this activity took... The orchestrator passes it to `next_activity` as `exit`", and line 73 says "Do not omit these fields". `exitDestinations` has exactly one production call site — validation.ts:45 — so removing this function leaves it with zero.

**Blast radius.** src/tools/workflow-tools.ts:50 (import) and 928 (the `buildValidation` argument); src/loaders/workflow-loader.ts:506 and its doc comment at 493 (which advertises it as the bare-list reader); tests/validation.test.ts:4 and its nine assertions at lines 59-146 (`describe('BF-04: validateActivityTransition warning strings')`); tests/workflow-loader.test.ts:309, 320, 325, 394-395 for `exitDestinations`. The `!view.act` initial-activity arm needs a home — the natural one is the transition handler's own resolution step 1, which already distinguishes "no entry on the first call of a session" from "named and absent".

**If left alone.** Two advisory checks warning about one mistake in two wordings, both of which must independently learn to flatten a three-form destination, and one of which is dead on every branch return because a refusal precedes it. That is the prohibition-shaped duplication the repo's own rule names: retire one and the second flattening implementation goes with it. Left in place it also acquires a new false positive — the initial-activity warning firing on a completed session, because "nothing in flight" and "nothing has run yet" stop being distinguishable in a scalar-free record.

## REMOVE — src/loaders/workflow-loader.ts:506 `exitDestinations(workflow, from)` versus src/utils/activity-variables.ts:540 `activityGraph(workflow)`

**What it does today.** Both answer "the activities an activity can reach". `exitDestinations` dedups `getExitBindings(...).map(b => b.to)`, going through the activity's declared exits. `activityGraph` builds the whole map at once with `[...new Set(Object.values(workflow.graph?.[activity.id] ?? {}))]`, reading the raw graph object.

The fan turns a latent duplicate into a maintained one: each must independently learn to flatten three destination forms, and the delivery plan schedules exactly that in two different stages — stage 1 adds "the flattening reachable-activities helper" to the loader, stage 2 makes "the graph builder in `src/utils/activity-variables.ts` flatten, with the de-duplication placed *after* the flatten". Two flatten-and-dedup implementations, one of which has a subtle ordering requirement the other does not.

**Evidence.** Definitions at workflow-loader.ts:506-508 and activity-variables.ts:540-546. Production call sites, measured by grepping `exitDestinations(` and `activityGraph(` across the tree: `exitDestinations` — one, src/utils/validation.ts:45; `activityGraph` — one, scripts/check-activity-variables.ts:233. Test assertions: tests/workflow-loader.test.ts:309, 320, 325, 394-395 and tests/activity-variables.test.ts:184. The ordering difference is real and tested — workflow-loader.test.ts:394-395 asserts `['next','other']` versus `['other','next']` for two workflows that differ only in exit declaration order, because `getExitBindings` iterates `activity.exits` while `activityGraph` iterates graph keys — but no consumer depends on it: validation.ts:48 uses `.includes`, and `unreachableReads` intersects over sources (activity-variables.ts:636-639), taking the first only as a candidate seed.

**Blast radius.** Removing `exitDestinations` follows the removal of `validateActivityTransition` above — that is its only production caller, so it drops to zero.
