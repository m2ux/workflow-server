### Research

- **RS-2 validated then adopted — the one scope change after confirmation.** `github-slugger`'s strip rule turned out to be a Unicode General-Category remove-list, so both [D-5](deferred-items.md) fidelity gaps are one-line fixes needing no dependency. The user reversed the deferral at the research-convergence gate: In Scope items 10–11, SC-11. The original deferral was sound on its evidence and wrong on its premise — it assumed fidelity was expensive.
- **RS-3 invalidated, and it is the sharpest finding of the activity.** "Seed the shared slugger from the guard" ([DP-6](#log)) imports a defect: the guard's `-N` counter omits `github-slugger`'s skip loop, so it under-generates anchors in any file holding a literal `-N`-shaped slug. Harmless while guard-only (a false `missing-anchor`); this package promotes it to the runtime resolver, where it becomes a resolution bug in exactly the class SC-1 exists to close. Zero corpus occurrences, so it would have shipped silently.
- **RS-4 invalidated** — `.trim()` is really CommonMark's ATX content rule, not part of slugging. `github-slugger` deliberately does not trim. Left in the slug function it makes `## Foo ##` slug as `foo-` and makes the module untestable against the reference fixtures.
- **RS-5 partially validated** — SC-1's "one slug implementation" is satisfiable, but the unit actually worth sharing is the **anchor table**, because `-N` resolution is a whole-file property and `ambiguous-anchor` needs base-slug multiplicity. One table serves four criteria at once and removes 2 of the codebase's 4 heading parsers.
- **RS-6 invalidated** — the obvious ambiguity predicate ("table holds `A` and `A-1`") false-positives on a file with headings `Echo` and `Echo 1`. The base-slug predicate is correct and returns exactly [RE-3](#log)'s 7 links, no others.
- **RS-7 validated by measurement, where RE-5 had only assumed it.** Fence hardening and the seven `-1` repoints are coupled in principle; measured, the corrected tracker changes anchor tables **purely additively** and re-points zero `-N` anchors.
- **RS-10 invalidated** (evidence-strength) — "more CommonMark-correct is uniformly better" is false for setext headings: a naive setext probe returns 664 hits, all YAML frontmatter closers, and 0 real ones. Today's blindness to setext is accidentally correct.
- **RS-12 corrected** (rejected-paths) — In Scope items 1 and 2 contradicted each other on whether the guard keeps its own slugger. Resolved against the user's D2 decision and fixed in the requirements, because the contradiction would have led the plan to fail SC-1's own grep check.
- **RS-1 invalidated** — the knowledge base holds nothing relevant. Worth recording rather than passing over: for a question that reduces to pinning an external implementation's exact behaviour, primary sources plus corpus measurement are the stronger evidence base, and the KB pass was never likely to be the one that mattered.
- **Evidence-strength notes:** the two weakest claims were checked rather than asserted. "Zero live referencers of the 23 divergent anchors" was verified beyond the corpus by grepping `docs/`, `src/`, `tests/` and `scripts/` (none found); "emphasis and code spans are slug-neutral" was checked against the corpus's single `*` heading, where the asterisk is a glob inside a code span and both sluggers strip it identically. The one claim still resting on a reimplementation rather than project tooling is SC-9's +1 count (RS-8), explicitly handed to implementation-analysis as [RC-4](04-kb-research.md#open-research-candidates).
- **Research takeaway:** every divergence research newly found — tab deletion, ATX closing sequences, indented headings, the missing skip loop, blockquote and list-item headings — is **latent at zero corpus occurrences**, and that is the point rather than a reason to skip them. This package's whole justification is preventing the next occurrence, not repairing the current two; a divergence with zero occurrences is the same shape as the 75-heading gap that justified the work in the first place. The corpus census bounds them all: a closed 26-character set, zero non-ASCII letters, zero tabs.

### Implementation Analysis

- **IA-3 invalidated, and it is the sharpest finding of the activity.** [RE-5](#log)/SC-9 asked for a count of newly exposed *links*, and the count is right (+1, [D-2](deferred-items.md), confirmed against the shipped guard in both directions). But diffing verdicts answers "what does CI say", not "what does the agent receive": through the real delivery path, fence hardening **truncates 2 authored template refs** by 1,009 and 3,022 chars, silently. [RS-7](#log)'s "0 `-N` anchors re-pointed" was correct and is reconfirmed here with the shipped `collectAnchors` — it simply never covered section *windows*. The generalisable lesson is one step past research's: when strengthening a guard whose logic is about to be shared with a resolver, diff the resolver's **output**, not only the guard's verdict.
- **IA-4 invalidated as stated, and it inverts an in-scope premise.** Research's "0 corpus occurrences" for ATX-indented headings holds only under a correct fence tracker; under today's naive toggle, polarity inversion exposes `  # Before` / `  # After` as level-1 headings. So ATX-indentation correctness (In Scope item 12's territory) is free **iff** fence hardening lands with it, and strictly harmful alone — which contradicts RE-5's "nothing else in scope depends on the outcome". The 2×2 attribution is in [05-implementation-analysis.md](05-implementation-analysis.md).
- **IA-1 and IA-2 invalidated together — the merge gate was measured against the wrong baseline.** The suite is red on **18** tests across 7 files, not 1, and the same 18 reproduce with the corpus exported at HEAD's recorded pointer, so neither this package nor the 9-commit submodule drift causes them. Two of the red files are this package's own regression nets (`reference-delivery.test.ts` for the `resource:` ledger, `mcp-server.test.ts` for `get_resource`) — and 3 of those failures are a stale fixture (`meta/activity-worker-prompt` no longer exists), i.e. minutes of work to restore the net on the exact tool being changed.
- **IA-6 validated — the byte-neutrality claim survives contact with the real delivery code.** With the two fence/ATX levers held at today's behaviour, the entire slug unification changes **0** of 1,333 resolved-ref hashes and regresses none, while turning 99 currently-unresolvable refs green. That is what makes G1/G2 cleanly attributable rather than lost in noise, and it is the strongest single result of the activity: comprehension Q2 is now measured through `loadResourceDelivery`, not inferred.
- **IA-7 invalidated** — comprehension's plan to close Q8 with `bench:token` cannot work: the harness walks `work-package` and the refs are in `prism-evaluate` (0 `prism` refs in the run). The answer by ledger arithmetic is also smaller and friendlier than the question implied — **3** slots not 7, and a **−1,003-char saving**, not a cost.
- **IA-8 invalidated in both directions** — [RE-7](#log) was wrong twice about the same pair of links: one is `get_resource`-verifiable after all (so SC-2 under-specifies its own verification), and the other silently loads a *different* `README.md` than its link target, exposing a latent id collision in which `resources/README.md` shadows every sibling.
- **IA-11 confirmed by the user, and the measurement taken after the decision made it cheaper than presented.** The challenge pass found a third fence option the record did not contain (harden the guard's tracker only: +1 verdict, 0 SC-5 violations, at the cost of two fence trackers), plus the stakeholder dimension the remedy carries (widening the fences changes rendered output on github.com in two files owned outside this package). The user chose option (a) — harden both surfaces and remedy the corpus — accepting that cost. Re-measuring the *remedied* corpus then showed **SC-9's `+1` collapses to `0`**: the newly exposed [D-2](deferred-items.md) link lives inside `architecture-summary.md`'s template body, so widening the outer fence returns it to fenced territory. The fence work therefore lands with **no verdict cost at all**, D-2's deferral is undisturbed, and the total content cost is 6 refs at exactly +2 bytes. The lesson is the mirror of IA-3's: having measured the guard's verdict and the resolver's output separately, the *interaction* with the remedy had to be measured too — the fix moves the input the guard reads.
- **IA-12 partially validated** (evidence-strength) — the three headline answers rest on the shipped guard and the real delivery path, but the SC-5 *predictions* run through a prospective ~90-line target-semantics module, and 3 of the 18 failure attributions are non-independent because two fragment test files hardcode `../workflows` instead of honouring `WORKFLOWS_DIR`. Both caveats are stated in the artifact rather than left implicit.
- **Evidence-strength notes:** the activity deliberately re-measured what research had already measured — the +1 count, the `-N` independence, the 23 link-in-heading divergences, the 26-character census — using project code rather than reimplementations, and every figure reproduced. What did *not* reproduce was the framing around those figures (IA-3, IA-4), which is the pattern worth carrying: research's numbers were sound and its **scope of measurement** was too narrow.
- **Analysis takeaway:** the two measurement tasks handed to this activity were phrased as confirmations, and both turned into corrections at one remove from the number they asked for. SC-9 asked for a link count and got the right one, but the decision it gates turns on delivered bytes nobody had counted. Q8 asked for a benchmark run and got the finding that the benchmark cannot see the refs in question. In both cases the question was well-formed against the *guard's* view of the corpus and the *default* workflow, and the exposure lived just outside both. Where a measurement is a gate, check what the instrument cannot see before trusting what it reports.

- **Takeaway:** the issue's two defects are unequally evidenced. Defect 1 is measured, live, and cheap. Defect 2 is well-designed, but by the end of the challenge pass every case the issue cites for it turned out reachable three other ways — AC 1's dedupe, a three-heading rename, or an ambiguity error — while the one consumer that genuinely wants hierarchy sits behind the issue's own out-of-scope line. The pattern to watch is inheriting a proposal's framing when its own fix-first advice already covers the evidence.
