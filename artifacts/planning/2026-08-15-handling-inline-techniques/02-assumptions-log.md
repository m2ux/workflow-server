## Open Assumptions

### PL-1: What a folded closure contains
**Assumption:** A folded callee delivers the operation body alone, so a group container body is not a closure member and the callee's container rules do not join the caller's obligation set.  
**Decision space:** Deliver the operation body only — the caller's obligations stay inside container trees it is already in, and the delivery rule agrees with the counting grammar, which collapses a qualified `group::op` pair to the one operation it names. Or deliver container bodies too, so a callee arrives with its full inherited contract, at the cost of importing rules from trees the caller is not in. The two differ by 2 closure members and about 6,200 bytes on the heaviest caller.  
**Why not code-resolvable:** A design decision with no recorded position, carried from [RC-5](04-kb-research.md#open-research-candidates). Both options are implementable and the code says nothing about which is intended.  
**Technical context:** The heaviest caller's obligation set is 2 rule entries today, both from its own two ancestor containers. Delivering container bodies brings 9 further own-rule entries plus 3 from two ancestor containers its own ancestry does not include — 14 in total, a sevenfold increase. The same choice fixes what SC-7 asserts: 40,671 bytes over 12 members without container bodies, 46,865 over 14 with them, both inside the 640,000-character budget.  
**Agent's position:** Deliver the operation body alone. It settles three questions at once — closure contents, SC-7's asserted figure, and rule propagation — and it is the only option under which the counting grammar and the delivery rule say the same thing about the same two-link form.  
**Reversibility:** easily-reversible — the traversal's member test, plus the figure SC-7 asserts.

### PL-2: Which counter a folded body draws on
**Assumption:** A folded body is charged to the unbudgeted operations-bundle channel rather than the budgeted eager channel.  
**Decision space:** Charge the operations bundle, so the eleven attributed baseline entries retire onto the channel already serving them, no new parameter is needed on the orchestrator door, and nothing can drop content — at the cost of growing the bundle whose serialised size seeds the eager counter. Or charge the budgeted eager channel, which uses the one real budget but moves those eleven entries onto a channel that drops content at first overflow, an unflagged regression path. Or add a budget parameter to the orchestrator door, which is a contract change to a door that has never carried one.  
**Why not code-resolvable:** The [RC-2](04-kb-research.md#open-research-candidates) and [RC-3](04-kb-research.md#open-research-candidates) question. Both channels' behaviour is established from the source; which one a folded body belongs to is a design choice.  
**Technical context:** The eager counter opens at the operations bundle's serialised size, so the two channels are coupled: the uncapped one draws down the capped one before any step technique is bundled, and the seed is measured after unchanged-marker collapse, making eager coverage delivery-history dependent. The orchestrator door takes no `context_tokens` and no `agent_id`, and collapses the whole bundle under one content hash rather than keying per technique.  
**Agent's position:** Charge the operations bundle. The retirement is then like-for-like — the compensation and its replacement ride the same channel — which is the only option under which SC-10 cannot outrun SC-7 for those eleven entries.  
**Reversibility:** path-committing — the orchestrator door's contract and the retirement order both follow from it.

### PL-3: Whether one classifier owns the link space
**Assumption:** The shared grammar module owns the technique-versus-resource link partition rather than leaving the collision to follow-ups.  
**Decision space:** Unify now, so two spellings of one call cannot classify differently and the new scan cannot inherit the existing predicate's blind spot. Or scope the module to technique references only and leave the partition to follow-ups, keeping this change smaller but building the second scan on a predicate that already claims strings the parse-time rewrite deliberately leaves alone.  
**Why not code-resolvable:** The [RC-4](04-kb-research.md#open-research-candidates) scope decision. The collision and its live instance are established; whether it is in this package is a scope call.  
**Technical context:** The claiming predicate excludes a link by a leading dot, a double colon, or more than one slash, so a bare one-slash technique path is claimed as a resource id while its dot-prefixed spelling is not. The module is built either way under SC-2, so the marginal cost is the partition's own tests; SC-2's single-definition clause requires no anchor slugger but does not require one classifier over the link space.  
**Agent's position:** Unify now. The blind spot is spelling-dependent, which makes it invisible to exactly the guard SC-3 exists to make total, and the module that would fix it is already this package's deliverable.  
**Reversibility:** easily-reversible — the classifier's remit and one caller.
