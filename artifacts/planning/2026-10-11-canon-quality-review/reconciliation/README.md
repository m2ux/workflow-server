# Independent reconciliation

The AP candidate authors and their coordinator reviewer are distinct. The principle candidate author is the coordinator; `/root/canon_contracts` independently reviews all 20 proposals against the captured sources. The coordinator accepts that review's five confirmations, thirteen narrowings and two rejections. Original candidates remain immutable in their primary review folders.

The coordinator reads each AP candidate's problem, witness, replacement and observing check, then reads the relevant captured source entries. `inspect_review.py` preserves the bounded projection method. The `ap-*.json` files record explicit judgments; they are not a programmatic semantic score. The renderer combines those judgments with the independently reviewed principle records. Proposed changes remain suggestions.

Narrowing matters: an entry's exception does not authorize its form under all other rules; cumulative constraints are not conflicting merely because they differ; an existing positive definition can settle a metaphor; and an outcome-specific exception can settle a broad heuristic. The retained judgments state those limits rather than count every first-pass concern as a defect.

Implementation-dependent input claims were checked against the pinned engine. The exact input wording is in the [implementation check](../reviews/ap_structures/implementation-check.md). Its AP-57 renderer premise remains explicitly unverified. The independently reviewed qualitative recommendations require no invented numeric threshold or claim of runtime execution.

The final artifact validator initially encountered its own not-yet-written result link, literal example hyperlinks rendered as navigable links, and a dictionary-versus-key counting bug. The validator and example presentation were corrected; the final run verifies 212 ledger rows, 20 independent principle dispositions, 66 exact independent quotes, both repository pins and 923 report links. These checks establish artifact integrity and evidence identity, not the truth of natural-language judgments by themselves.
