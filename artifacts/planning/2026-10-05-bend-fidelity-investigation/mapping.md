# Mechanism Table and Trust Boundary

Every mechanism the server, the guards and the definition formats use to keep an agent's claim checkable, and what each becomes under Bend. Ground truth: server `origin/main` at `ab680d9d`, corpus `origin/workflows` at `15860ff3`, Bend `565d7fde` (2.0.35). Reader maps with file and line citations for every row are under [notes/maps/](notes/maps/); the spike that produced the measured rows is under [spike/](spike/README.md).

Vocabulary. **Refuses**: the call or load fails. **Warns**: the call proceeds and a warning is recorded. **Records**: an event is written and nothing is compared. **Checker**: `bend2/bend.ts`, the TypeScript type checker, whose soundness is stated as "trusted claims" (`bend2/bend.ts:230-232`). **Kernel**: `bend2/bendtt.lean`, the Lean-proven BendTT checker behind `--verdict`, reached through the unproven elaborator `bend2/safe.ts`. **Proven** below means the kernel accepted the law; **refused** means the checker refuses the broken definition or call as a type error.

## Part 2. The trust boundary

Each bullet of `docs/fidelity.md` Limits of Detectability (`fidelity.md:427-434`), classified. **Moves**: Bend makes it refused or proven. **Narrows**: Bend shrinks what an agent can fake. **Inherent**: no type system reaches it. Each classification names what the guarantee would rest on.

| # | Limit | Class | Why, and what is left | Rests on | Cheapest alternative |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | Step execution is not provable | **narrows** | The report becomes a typed term: a step omitted, a required slot absent, or an undeclared output key is a type error, not a warning (`neg_manifest_missing`, `neg_manifest_absent_value`, `report_type()`). What is left is everything that matters: whether the work was done and whether the output values are true. A type checker proves properties of a term, and the term is text the agent wrote. The "evidence" of P3 is a claim with a well-formed shape | checker for the shape (the kernel confirms types this small: `mvw/out/mvw.verdict.txt`) | a Zod schema generated per activity, refusing |

## Part 3. Where each Bend guarantee rests

The brief asks that no guarantee be oversold. The chain under `--verdict` has three links (kernel map):

1. **The checker** (`bend2/bend.ts`, 3,882 lines). Its header says the file was "99% human-designed and audited" (`bend.ts:1-3`); the README says "The checker has no proof and may have bugs" and that the **compiler** (not the kernel) is "99% AI-written" (`README.md` Limitations). Its metatheory is stated as "trusted claims" (`bend.ts:230-232`). The CHANGELOG records four closed proofs of `Empty` fixed between 2.0.17 and 2.0.25, two gate-integrity fixes, and acceptance that widened or narrowed in five releases, over 36 releases in 17 days (`CHANGELOG.md`; proof-authoring map).
2. **The elaborator** (`bend2/safe.ts`, 1,548 lines, AI-written, unproven). It translates the checked book to BendTT text; "the translation has no proof, so read it to confirm a law" (`GUIDE.md:328-332`). A def with no body goes out `opaque` at a synthetic model: every `F32` operation, every Base IO effect, and the handle types reach the kernel as stand-ins (kernel map facts on `hello.bendtt` and `f32use.bendtt`).
3. **The kernel** (`bend2/bendtt.lean`, 3,892 lines, 0 `sorry`). It proves `Claim.consistent`: no def of a checked book has a type convertible to the empty enum (`bendtt.lean:1529-1534`), plus soundness, confluence, subject reduction, progress, halting (`:1536-1560`). Its parser is outside the claims. Its `FUEL` is 400,000,000 steps per check (`:143-145`), which the spike's two bag-walk laws exceed (`real/perlaw/out/reads_resolve.verdict.txt`: "out of fuel", 7.9 s; the checker accepts the same law in 0.9 s).

So, per spike law:

| Law | Checker | Kernel | What a reader can conclude |
|---|---|---|---|
| MVW: graph totality, gate typing, manifest typing, session affinity, two `{==}` laws | accepts, 0.11 s | accepts, 0.13 s | proven, given the elaborator translated faithfully |
| Real: `loops_bounded`, `option_exits_declared`, `writes_produced`, `reads_used` | accepts, 0.13–0.28 s | accepts, 0.26–1.04 s | proven, same caveat |
| Real: `reads_resolve`, `no_orphan_input` | accepts, 0.7–0.9 s | **out of fuel**, 6.9–7.9 s | rests on the AI-supported checker alone |
| Any law over a program using `@unsafe` or a user foreign effect | names every tainted def and fails (`probe7_unsafe`) | never runs | not a proof |
| Any law about IO or `F32` | accepts | accepts against a synthetic model | proves nothing about the effect |

The practical reading: for the fidelity system, the kernel confirms the small type-level laws (graph, gate, manifest shape) and does not confirm the dataflow walks over a real activity, which are exactly the laws that would retire guard family C. Those would rest on a young checker whose acceptance changed in five of the last 36 releases.
