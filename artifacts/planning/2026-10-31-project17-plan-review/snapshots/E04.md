## Overview

A board is read back as a standup. The summary opens with a paragraph a manager can read, lists each initiative with its epics and their tasks, names the few items that are next, and marks every item with one symbol for its state. A key explains the symbols, and the whole block pastes into Slack as written.

## Problem

- **Nothing to report from.**
  A board said where each item stood, but producing a status update from it meant reading every column and writing the account by hand each time.
- **No management line.**
  A summary of item states gave no account of what had been accomplished, so a reader outside the work could not tell what the period delivered.
- **Epics too coarse.**
  An epic line said an epic was in progress without saying which piece of work, so the summary did not answer what anyone was doing.
- **Every ready item listed.**
  Listing everything at Ready swamped the message, so the items that mattered next were lost in it.
- **Competing marks.**
  More than one symbol could stand for one state, so two summaries of the same board read differently and no key could explain either.
- **Slack formatting assumed.**
  Links were written in a form only a bot can post, so a summary pasted by a person showed its own markup.

## Proposal

- **A standup from the board.**
  One summary covers the whole board: each initiative, its epics, and each epic's tasks.
- **A paragraph for management.**
  The summary opens with a plain-language paragraph on what was accomplished, above the board line.
- **One mark per state.**
  Each state has exactly one symbol, each initiative is marked by its state, and the next task of an epic and each Next item carry the ready mark.
- **A short Next list.**
  Only the few highest-priority items at Ready appear under Next.
- **A key.**
  The summary closes with a key naming what each symbol and each letter stands for.
- **Pasteable links.**
  Each issue and pull request is named with its bare URL, the form that survives a person pasting the message.
