## Overview

This epic brings the checks up to what the server serves. A corpus guard resolves the roots its invocation names rather than one tree, and reports its findings over all of them. The collision guard is the one this matters most to: a name two roots claim is exactly the fault multi-root serving introduces, and a guard that sees one root at a time cannot see it. With the guards measuring the union, the reservation of a public name against private reuse stops being a rule written in prose and becomes a finding.

## Problem

A guard resolves one root, so a fault that spans two is outside what any check measures.

- **Root resolution answers with one tree.**
  [`requireWorkflowsRoot`](https://github.com/m2ux/workflow-server/blob/62ee9612c0649c0a99653cdf2ab2ff5469b43be6/guards/workflows-root.ts#L178) returns a single root, and the corpus guards each take `--root` for one tree.
- **A collision spanning roots is unmeasured.**
  [`check-namespace-collision`](https://github.com/m2ux/workflow-server/blob/62ee9612c0649c0a99653cdf2ab2ff5469b43be6/guards/check-namespace-collision.ts) indexes the root it is given, so two directories claiming one name from different roots are two clean runs.
- **A served corpus is wider than a checked one.**
  Every guard pointed at one root leaves the definitions of every other served root unchecked, including the rules those definitions are subject to.

## Proposal

- **Root resolution takes the list.**
  A guard resolves every root its invocation names, by the same spelling the server's configuration uses.
- **A guard measures every root it resolves.**
  Each corpus guard indexes the union and reports against it, so a finding is raised wherever the defect sits.
- **A cross-root claim is a finding.**
  A namespace name claimed in two roots is reported by the collision guard, which is the check that replaces reserving public names against private reuse by convention.
