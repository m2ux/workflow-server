## Overview

This epic works the concurrency backlog a survey of the corpus produced: thirty-one places that take work one unit at a time, each classified and ordered. It starts with a decision rather than a migration, because two rules disagree about whether an activity may fan out at all. That decision is taken: an activity's exit declares the fan and the orchestrator executes it on the activity's behalf. The rest is two items independent of the answer, a migration list to re-derive against the construct the answer names, and two sites that each wait on a decision of their own. Sequential execution is never wrong, so nothing here is a correctness fault in the concurrency itself — what the corpus pays is wall-clock it need not, an independence judgement it makes and then discards, and two defects that pay off whatever the decisions conclude.

## Problem

The corpus takes work one unit at a time in thirty-one places. A survey classified every one of them and ordered a backlog: eight hold work that is independent on the definitions as they stand, four need a cross-iteration read or a shared write resolved first, seventeen cannot fan out for a structural reason, and two already run work concurrently on prose alone. This epic works that backlog.

**Two rules disagree about where fan-out is allowed.**
An activity's steps execute in a spawned worker, and a worker dispatches no workers of its own — a conduct rule, stated twice and cited five times. The pattern library's own guidance says the opposite: that its activities cover fan-out and consolidation *inside* an activity. Six activity definitions across three workflows bind the shared dispatch operation from a step, which is written against the first claim and runs under the second. All six reach its sequential branch at run time. That is conformance rather than breakage, and it is why no site in the corpus fans out today. A seventh binding would not change that.

**The one kind of concurrency the corpus performs has no name.**
A toolchain validation technique starts four concurrent shell invocations in a single caller's turn, waits for all four, gathers their results in a fixed order, and falls back to sequential execution under resource pressure. Nothing names that structure. The shared primitive that does exist dispatches agents rather than shells, so it does not cover this case. Four elements are already written into the technique's own protocol — work-unit shape, combine hook, wait-for-all, degrade path — so the open question is whether they become a shared contract or stay one technique's local prose.

**A workflow decides which units are independent and throws the judgement away.**
One analysis planner declares an output naming which units can run concurrently, and its plan template reserves a line for it. The machine-readable unit list beside it carries no concurrency field, no activity or workflow file in that workflow mentions concurrency at all, and the four passes that consume the plan walk the units one at a time. It is the corpus's only recorded independence judgement and nothing reads it.

**A multi-target review keeps only its last target's findings.**
One quality-review loop writes three findings files under fixed names into a single planning folder on every iteration. A two-target review therefore overwrites the first target's three files with the second's. This loses findings today, sequentially, with no concurrency involved. Its sibling loop in the neighbouring workflow shows the alternative: accumulate into a register and write once.

## Proposal

**W01 — Decide where fan-out is owned. Answered.**
Of three answers, the second is taken: an activity may declare a fan its exit carries and the orchestrator executes on its behalf, naming the work units while the orchestrator dispatches. The two not taken are fan-out staying wholly with the orchestrator, which closes in-activity concurrency as a design option, and workers dispatching at depth two, which contradicts a rule stated twice and is recorded for completeness rather than recommended. W04 and W05 depend on the decision, which closes neither.

Host files state their own depth and nesting behaviour, or the depth rule stops citing them and carries the claim itself. That half is outstanding: the rule still refers a reader to the harness files for nesting limits and those files carry no depth statement, so the citation resolves to nothing. The two senses of "the sequential case" are distinguished wherever the reassurance is given: the fan-out primitive's in-context loop, which runs anywhere, and the mode-selecting operation's one-agent-per-brief branch, which needs the dispatch tool.

**W02 — Name shell concurrency, or record that it stays prose.**
The argument for leaving it local: one site needs it, the anti-pattern catalogue only flags a shared operation added where a shared capability already exists to duplicate, and a contract with a single caller cannot be validated against diversity. The argument against: it is the only concurrency the corpus actually performs. Either answer closes the item; recording the reason is what makes it closed rather than forgotten.

**W03 — Fix the two defects that pay regardless.**
The discarded concurrency plan resolves one of two ways — the field reaches the machine-readable unit list as a declared component the passes can read, or the plan stops claiming to produce it. The findings-file collision takes a per-target artifact name.

**W04 — Re-derive and migrate the ready list.**
Seven sites, ordered by benefit per unit of change. A site migrates to a graph destination naming the per-unit activity and the collection to run it over, which sets each migration's price and settles the restructure-versus-flag question by construction. Neither the in-activity decompose-brief-dispatch-gather-combine chain nor borrowing the orchestrator-workers pattern activity outright is a target. The list is re-derived against the corpus as it stands before any site is picked up. The largest fan in the corpus is around a hundred units; the smallest change for the largest fan is a set of evidence probes whose gather contract and combine step both already exist.

**W05 — Settle the blocked pair.**
Two sites, each needing a decision written before it can be estimated as work rather than as a question: whether an analysis pass is meant to read earlier units' artifacts, which two passes turn on together, and whether per-task worktree isolation is worth its price.

**Scope.**
The thirty-one sequential sites the survey classified, the two rules that disagree about in-activity fan-out, the host files that claim to hold nesting policy, the unnamed shell concurrency at its single call site, and the two defects W03 fixes. Definition-layer only: workflow, activity, technique and resource files, plus the planning records that hold the two decisions. A migration replaces a loop with a graph destination, so a loop step never takes a concurrency mode. The seventeen not-a-candidate sites are not re-classified: twelve hold a checkpoint or a dialogue that needs an answer before the next step, three trigger a child workflow per iteration, and two are the sequential member of a pattern pair whose concurrent sibling sits beside it. None becomes available under any outcome of W01.

## Acceptance Criteria

- [ ] **AC1.** A written decision records where fan-out is owned, names which of the three answers is taken and why, and the corpus states one claim about whether an activity may fan out.
- [ ] **AC2.** Host files state their own depth and nesting behaviour, or the depth rule carries the claim itself and cites no host file for it. That statement is evidenced against the actual tool surface, with the check recorded.
- [ ] **AC3.** The two senses of "the sequential case" are distinguished wherever the reassurance is given.
- [ ] **AC4.** Every activity-step binding of the shared dispatch operation above a concurrency of one is consistent with that decision — retired, retargeted, or documented as the sequential case with the reason.
- [ ] **AC5.** A written decision records whether shell concurrency becomes a shared contract or stays one technique's local prose, with the rationale for the choice.
- [ ] **AC6.** The declared concurrency plan either reaches a consumer that reads it or is absent from the planner's declared outputs.
- [ ] **AC7.** Each of the seven ready sites is either migrated, with its step list, variable contract and outcome statement updated together, or carries a recorded reason for not migrating under W01's answer.
- [ ] **AC8.** Each of the two blocked sites carries a written decision, and is then either migrated or recorded as closed against migration.
- [ ] **AC9.** A multi-target quality review keeps every target's findings files.
- [ ] **AC10.** The fan-out ownership guard fails any rule text or technique `## Rules` section outside the recorded fan-out home that states whether an activity may fan out.
